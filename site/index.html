Did the new recommender really win?

A simulated A/B test of a new recommendation engine. The headline metric improves, but the story changes once you separate novelty from steady state, split by user group, and look at what happens to long-tail items.

All data on this page is synthetic. Effect sizes (novelty decay, subgroup lifts, exposure concentration) are parameters I set in a simulator. The point is the analysis pipeline: it should recover the planted effects and raise the planted warnings. No claim is made about any real product.

1Is the lift real, or novelty?

The 14-day average is +7.5%, but the first three days run at +17.7% while days 8–14 run at +2.7%. A user-level cumulative view keeps early days in the average, so it looks healthy long after the effect has faded.

2Who is helped and who is hurt?

The overall number is an average of opposing effects: new users -4.6% (BH-adjusted p = 0.023) versus high-activity returning users +15.1%. Subgroup results are exploratory and corrected for multiple comparisons.

3What happens to the long tail?

Treatment concentrates impressions on already-popular items: Gini 0.68 → 0.81. Short-term click metrics would not show this; a guardrail metric does.

4Can the experiment be trusted at all?

Before reading any result, check the split. Here an injected logging loss (p = 2.2e-12) is flagged and traced to android; the clean run passes (p = 0.31).

Method

  • Estimand. Relative lift in 14-day GMV per user, treatment vs control, on the pre-registered 14-day window. Daily and cumulative views are diagnostics only: repeated looks inflate false positives.
  • Test. Two-sided Welch t-test; relative-lift CI by the delta method.
  • CUPED. Pre-period GMV as covariate; variance reduced by 27% overall. New users have no history, so it does nothing for them.
  • SRM. Chi-square goodness of fit to the intended 50/50 split, alert at p < 0.001, then sliced by device.
  • Subgroups. Exploratory; Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted across 9 tests.
  • Power. Planned before the experiment from baseline variance (below), not computed after the fact.

Does the pipeline work?

Repeated simulation (1000 runs of 30,000 users). With no planted effect, the false-positive rate is 5.9% (raw) and 5.5% (CUPED), and SRM alerts fire in 0.2% of runs. With planted effects, 95% intervals cover the known true lift about 95% of the time:

Limitations

  • Simulated users are independent; there is no interference between users, which real marketplaces violate (the cannibalization concern from the brief).
  • The recommender is not built: a click model stands in for it, so no claim is made about LLM or vector retrieval quality.
  • Planted effects are my assumptions. Recovering them shows the method works, not that real recommenders behave this way.
  • The SRM scenario is localized by device only; no funnel-stage decomposition.
